What and how does this tool work

Think of pixel art as graph paper. Take a sheet and, square by square, either fill a square in solid black or leave it white: no half-filled squares, no shading. Now blow that sheet up to the size of a wall or a utility box. Every big block you see on the mural is one square of that graph paper, and each square is exactly one pixel of the original image: filled or not, black or white. Pixel Extract works backwards: it finds the graph paper again and asks each square one question, filled or empty? Colour murals work the same way, the answer is just a colour instead of black or white.

  1. Flatten the paper (Straighten). A photo taken from below or off to the side turns the paper's neat rectangle into a slanted, tapering shape. The three sliders, the edge points and the Edges lines undo that tilt, so the grid lines on the art run straight up and level again.
  2. Lay the grid on top (Grid, Count, Edges). You tell Pixel Extract where the paper's border is and how many squares it has across and down. Counting dots, edge lines and Auto-detect all help line the yellow grid up with the art's real squares.
  3. Read each square's colour. For every square, Pixel Extract looks only at the middle of it and takes the median colour (on graph paper: is the middle black or white?), so grout lines, scratches, glare and the edges of neighbouring squares don't sneak in. Areas you marked as blocked are skipped; a square that's fully covered borrows from the squares around it.
  4. Shrink each square to one pixel. The colours are written out at one pixel per square, giving you the original underlying raster at 1:1. If you choose a palette, near-identical shades are merged back into the few paints the artist actually used.

Why it works: each painted square covers hundreds or thousands of camera pixels, so even with uneven light and a bit of noise there's plenty of evidence to vote on what its one true colour is. View adjustments only change what you see, never what gets read, unless you tell them to.
